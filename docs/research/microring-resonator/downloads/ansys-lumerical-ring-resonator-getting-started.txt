# Ring resonator getting started – Design and initial simulation
Source: https://optics.ansys.com/hc/en-us/articles/360042800293-Ring-resonator-getting-started-Design-and-initial-simulation
Publisher: Ansys (Lumerical)
Date accessed: 2026-06-18

## Summary
Part 1 of the ring resonator tutorial uses MODE to design and simulate a ring resonator. Free spectral range (FSR) and quality factor (Q factor) are key performance metrics for this silicon-on-insulator (SOI) based waveguide design targeting on-chip communication applications.

## Basic Configuration
The most basic configuration of the microring resonator consists of a ring-shaped waveguide coupled to two optical waveguides. The cavity mode is excited by evanescent coupling between closely spaced optical waveguides.

## Defining Quantities
- Average (effective) ring length L
- Complex propagation constant β of circulating mode
- Field transmission coefficients of waveguide coupler τ11 and τ12
- Bend loss

## Resonance Condition
On resonance, the phase acquired by the wave after a complete round-trip is an integer multiple of 2π:
βL = 2πN, with N as the mode number.

If effective refractive index is independent of wavelength:
L = N λ0 / neff
where neff = cβ/ω and λ0 = c/f0.

## Free Spectral Range (wavelength units)
FSR = λ² / (ng L)
where ng = c(dβ/dω) is the group index.

## Q Factor Approximation (neglecting losses other than coupling)
Q ≈ λ / (2 δλ) = (ng L π / λ) × (|τ11| / (1 - |τ11|²))

## Design Example
- Target WDM channel spacing: 200 GHz (1.6 nm at 1550 nm)
- Drop every 16th channel => FSR = 3200 GHz (25.6 nm at 1550 nm)
- Desired FWHM: 100 GHz => Q ≈ 1550 nm / 0.8 nm ≈ 2000
- SOI waveguide: 400 nm wide, 180 nm high
- Group index at 1550 nm: ≈ 4.63
- Calculated ring length L ≈ 20.2 µm
- Coupling gap 100 nm gives Δn ≈ 0.109 and coupling length ≈ 1427 nm; in practice Lc = 0 and coupling from bent section is enough.
- Radius chosen: 3.1 µm

## Key Practical Notes
- High-Q devices require longer simulation times for fields to decay.
- Precise resonance position is very sensitive to exact optical length of the ring.
- 3D FDTD shows lower total transmission because it accounts for more loss sources.
