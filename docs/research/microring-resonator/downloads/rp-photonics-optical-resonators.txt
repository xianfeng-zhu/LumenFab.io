# Optical resonators
Source: https://www.rp-photonics.com/optical_resonators.html
Author: Dr. Rüdiger Paschotta (RP Photonics)
Date accessed: 2026-06-18

## Definition
Arrangements of optical components which allow a beam of light to circulate. Also called optical cavities.

## Key Concepts
- Resonators with bulk components vs. waveguide resonators.
- Linear resonators vs. ring resonators.
- Stable vs. unstable bulk-optical resonators.
- Resonator modes: self-consistent field distributions reproducing after each round trip.
- Resonant enhancement: build-up of high intracavity power at resonance.
- Free spectral range (FSR): frequency spacing between adjacent longitudinal modes.
- Finesse: ratio of FSR to resonance bandwidth.
- Q-factor: ratio of resonance frequency to bandwidth.

## Waveguide Resonators
In waveguide resonators, transverse mode structure is determined by waveguide properties and is constant everywhere if waveguide properties are constant. A single transverse mode may exist, e.g., in single-mode fibers.

## Ring vs Linear
- Linear resonators: light bounces back and forth; standing wave.
- Ring resonators: light circulates in a closed loop; no end mirrors; can circulate in two directions.
- Ring resonators avoid direct back-reflection to source and spatial hole burning when unidirectional.

## Micro- and Nano-resonators
Very small resonators (few microns or less). Examples:
- Short standing-wave resonators with Bragg mirrors (DFB lasers, VCSELs).
- Tiny ring resonators on photonic integrated circuits (e.g., silicon-on-insulator).
- Toroidal micro-resonators and whispering-gallery-mode microspheres.
Small resonators have a large free spectral range.

## Applications
- Laser resonators
- Etalons / optical filters
- Mode cleaner cavities
- Optical frequency standards
- Precision length measurement
- Enhancement cavities for nonlinear optics
- Cavity ring-down spectroscopy
- Dispersion effects (Gires–Tournois interferometer)
