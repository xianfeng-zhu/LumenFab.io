## 2. TFLN Penetration Rate in 1.6T Modules

### Current Estimates (2026)

| Technology | 1.6T Penetration (2026E) | Notes |
|------------|-------------------------|-------|
| **SiPh (Silicon Photonics)** | **60-80%** | Dominant due to EML capacity shortage; CW laser + SiPh modulator is lower cost |
| **InP EML** | Declining in pure modulation | Still required for **laser sources** (unavoidable); EML modulator capacity severely constrained |
| **TFLN (Thin-Film Lithium Niobate)** | **>20% (high-end)** | 2026 = volume production inflection year; in long-reach/high-performance 1.6T segments, share is >20-30% |

### At Higher Speeds

| Generation | TFLN Share (Est.) |
|------------|-------------------|
| 1.6T (2026) | 15-20% overall; >30% in high-end |
| 3.2T (2028+) | **>40% projected**; could reach 40-70% |
| CPO light engines | Approaching **100% in some designs** (TFLN's low Vpi and bandwidth are unmatched here) |

### Key Architecture Insight

The industry consensus is **not "TFLN vs. SiPh vs. InP" but "InP light source + TFLN modulator + SiPh integration platform."** These are complementary, not substitutive:
- **InP**: Light emission (unavoidable for now)
- **TFLN**: Ultra-high-speed modulation (>100 GHz bandwidth, <2V drive)
- **SiPh**: Integration platform for passive routing, splitting, coupling

### 1.6T Shipment Volume Context

| Source | 2025 | 2026 | 2027 |
|--------|------|------|------|
| **Cignal AI** (Jan 2026) | Small base | **>5M units -> revised to ~10M** | Rapid growth |

| **Chinese industry tracking** | ~250K units | ~20M units (possibly includes modules at sub-component level) | 1.6T = 800G equally split |

If 1.6T reaches 10M units in 2026 and TFLN has 15-20% share, that's **1.5-2M TFLN-based 1.6T modules**, each containing 1-2 modulators = **1.5-4M TFLN chips** just from this segment.

### TFLN's Role in CPO

- **No precise TFLN-specific CPO share % published** in any source found.
- TFLN is viewed as the **modulator of choice for external CPO architectures** (especially for Chinese supply chain, blocked from TSMC COUPE).
- For **scale-up CPO** (highest-value segment), microring resonators + TSMC COUPE will dominate -- TFLN is NOT the primary approach here.
- For **scale-out CPO** (rack-to-rack), TFLN/LPO approaches will capture meaningful share in 2026-2027.
- **JP Morgan/LightCounting** sees TFLN as a key CPO enabler; the $11B 2030 CPO market estimate supports significant TFLN content.

- **Quarterly run-rate**: Potentially $2B/quarter by 2028

### TFLN's Role in Next-Gen OCS

- TFLN modulators enable **higher-speed OCS-switched links** (1.6T/3.2T per port).
- Current OCS uses MEMS mirrors for purely optical switching -- TFLN isn't in the switch fabric itself, but in the **transceivers connected to OCS ports**.
- Next-gen OCS (~2028) could integrate TFLN-based **wavelength-selective switches** or **fast-reconfiguration modulators** for sub-microsecond OCS reconfiguration.

### What Fraction Needs >100 GHz Bandwidth?

**The >100 GHz bandwidth modulator segment is the fastest-growing at 19.65% CAGR**, driven by:
1. **1.6T transceivers** (100 Gbaud+ per lane)
2. **3.2T transceivers** (200 Gbaud+ per lane)
3. **Coherent-lite / LPO** architectures
4. **CPO light engines**

Based on the speed-tier shipment estimates:
- In 2025: ~42M high-speed (400G+) modules shipped. Of these, maybe **5-10%** require >100 GHz modulators (early 1.6T, high-end coherent).
- In 2028: With 1.6T at ~30M units and 3.2T beginning, the share requiring >100 GHz could reach **40-50%** of all high-speed modules.
