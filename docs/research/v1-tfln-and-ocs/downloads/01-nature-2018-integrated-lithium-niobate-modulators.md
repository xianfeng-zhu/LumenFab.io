# Integrated lithium niobate electro-optic modulators operating at CMOS-compatible voltages

**Source:** Nature, Volume 562, Pages 101-104 (2018)
**URL:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-018-0551-y
**Authors:** Cheng Wang, Mian Zhang, Xi Chen, Maxime Bertrand, Amirhassan Shams-Ansari, Sethumadhavan Chandrasekhar, Peter Winzer, Marko Loncar
**Type:** Peer-reviewed research paper (Nature)
**Label:** Paper-S1

## Abstract Summary

The paper demonstrates monolithically integrated lithium niobate electro-optic modulators on a chip-scale platform, achieving CMOS-compatible drive voltages. This overcomes a key limitation of previous bulky lithium niobate modulators.

## Key Results

- Data rates up to **210 Gbps**
- Ultra-low on-chip optical loss: **<0.5 dB**
- CMOS-compatible drive voltages
- Achieved through engineering of microwave and photonic circuits for high electro-optical efficiency, ultra-low losses, and group-velocity matching
- Cited >1,500 times, marking a breakthrough in making lithium niobate viable for integrated photonics

## Significance

This is considered the seminal paper that launched the TFLN integrated photonics field. It demonstrated that lithium niobate — long the workhorse of the optoelectronic industry in bulk form — could be integrated at chip scale with performance that surpassed existing technologies.
